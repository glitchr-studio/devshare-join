DevShare privacy policy

19 October 2026

DevShare lets a developer share the websites and React Native apps they are building on their Mac with guests who open them on an iPhone, an iPad or another computer. This page says what the apps handle, where it goes and for how long. The short version: everything goes from the guest's device to the host's Mac and back; nothing is sent to GLITCH ART, and nothing is kept.

The pages themselves are served by the host's Mac and shown in the app's own browser. They are not stored by the app beyond the session: “Always fresh pages”, on by default, empties the browser's cache before each page. The host's websites may set cookies in that browser; they are discarded when you leave the session.

A React Native app the host shares runs inside the app, as Expo Go runs one: its code comes from the host's Mac, through the session, each time you open it, and is not kept. Its cookies and cache last only while it is open. What it does is the host's app's own doing, as a website's is.

The camera is used only to read a QR code, on the device. No picture leaves it.

What the Mac app handles

DevShare for Mac reads the projects you add to it on your own Mac, starts and stops them, and shares the addresses you choose. It shows the guests of a session by the name they gave and the pages they open.

It keeps, on your Mac only, in its own folder that only your user account can read: your settings; the history of your sessions (their projects, their guests' names and the pages they opened, and each invitation's key and code, so that the same QR code can be shared again); and your groups and arrangement of the list. “Clear history”, or deleting one session, removes them.

When you add a folder of a server, DevShare reaches it with your own SSH keys and settings. It copies, from each project there, only the few files that say what it shares (its Compose files, Makefile, devshare.toml and .env files, the lines naming a secret or holding a password left out), into its own folder on your Mac, and starts and stops the projects on that server when you ask. Nothing of it goes anywhere else.

Unless you turn it off in the settings, guests are shown what each shared project is made of: its technologies, the services of its Compose files and its direct dependencies with their versions.

Relays

When a guest cannot reach the host directly, the connection goes through a relay. The relay sees the two devices' network addresses and the amount of traffic, not its content, which is encrypted end to end between the guest and the host. By default DevShare uses the public relays run by number 0; a host or a guest can name a relay of their own instead, including one they run themselves.

What GLITCH ART receives

Nothing. DevShare has no account, no analytics and no advertising. The iPhone and iPad app includes open-source components (React Native, Hermes, Expo's modules) to run the apps a host shares; they send nothing to anyone. The invitation page at join.glitchr.dev reads the invitation on your device and sends nothing to a server. GLITCH ART does not see who shares, who joins, or what is shown.

Your rights

Under the General Data Protection Regulation you may ask what is held about you, and have it corrected or erased. Since GLITCH ART holds nothing, the host of a session is the one to ask about a session's guests. For anything else, write to support@glitchr.io. You may also complain to the CNIL, the French data protection authority.

Children

DevShare is a tool for people who develop websites and those they show them to. It is not addressed to children and collects nothing from anyone.
